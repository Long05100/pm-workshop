Input 1 · Project Fundamentals

What is the base of the project?

Goal

What are we trying to achieve?

For example higher margin, improve processes, improve customer satisfaction.  

Time

How much time and effort are we willing to put in this project?

Having Project X finshed by Date X.

Cost

What is our investment budget?

How much money do we need and how much do we actually want to spend?

Quality

What standards do we need to meet?

Special requirements needed?

Define goals for your project

Without goals you cannot measure your success or prioritize tasks. Having undefined goals can lead to complications in later decisions.

6

Input 2 · Requirements

What are the expectations?

Functional

What should the product be able to do?

Features that have to be included?

Deliverables

What exactly do we hand over at the end?

Meet expectations, avoid over-engineering.

Deadline

When does it have to be finished?

A date that all parties agree with.

When is it accepted as done?

The idea: define the acceptance criteria before starting. Write it down, so everyone knows when the project is actually finished. Setting up the requirements is crucial otherwise there is the risk your project never ends.

7

Input 3 · Scope

How do you pin the scope down?

Step 1

Define

What belongs to this project?

Requirements are set up so you can define your scope.

Step 2

Exclude

What clearly does not belong to this project?

Delete requirements which clearly do not belong to your scope.

Step 3

Align

Does everyone understand the scope?

Review them with a decision-maker.

Step 4

Record

Where is it written down?

Agreement, signed and dated by responsible person.

Project scope is your insurance

Every addition should go through the same four steps: define it, identify what it replaces, agree on it and document it. This prevents uncontrolled expansions of a project´s scope and their required time, cost and resources.

Input 4 · Feasibility

Can we actually build this?

Technical Skills

Does anyone here know how to do this part?

Know-how in house?

Tool Access

Do we have the right tools, software or connections?

The system, the data or the licence to work with.

Capacity

Do we have free man power?

Are there other projects running at the same time?

Risk

Any risks which can lead to failure?

Risk awareness and acceptance.

Checking feasibility ensures viability

Feasibility issues don't necessarily kill the project. But they may require an adjustment of scope and objectives.

9

Input 5 · Stakeholders

Who is deciding?

Power

Decision making authority.

Different stakeholders, different levels of authority.

Interest

Who actually cares about the result?

How interested or affected is the stakeholder by the project?

Influence

What is their Influence on the project?

What can they do in or for the project

Information

Who needs to hear what, and how often?

Different stakeholder receive different levels of information

Put it all in one stakeholder register

Know your stakeholders, understand their influence, and know what they need from you. That way, you know who to approach when you need information, input, support, or a decision.

Your job
